01 / THE STORY

Some stories
stay with you.

Long before I studied public policy, literature taught me to pay attention.

At school, poems such as Maithili Sharan Gupt’s Manushyata, Siyaram Sharan Gupt’s Ek Phool Ki Chah and Arun Kamal’s Khushboo Rachte Hain Haath, alongside Anees Jung’s Lost Spring, made social divides feel close. I recognised something of everyday life in them.

My grandmother’s newspaper habit became mine. Stories about ordinary people and their difficulties stayed with me long after I had put the paper down. I kept wondering about the circumstances behind them, and how hardship could become so familiar that we stopped noticing it.

I first built a career in finance and data research. During COVID, returning home gave me room to slow down and revisit those questions. I wanted to understand the patterns, contradictions and root causes behind people’s experiences—and learn alongside others asking similar questions.

That return led me towards political science, legislative work and field research. Today, I bring data, documents and conversations together, trying to understand public problems without losing sight of the people living through them.

ENVIRONMENT · COMMUNITIES · ORAL HISTORIES

Kiul Nadi

Independent research · Book in progress · 2026–present

A river is more than the water that runs through it.

I am writing narrative non-fiction about the ecological, cultural and socioeconomic life of the Kiul River system in Jharkhand. In Giridih’s Tisri block, my fieldwork follows drainage networks, community practices and changes linked to deforestation and sand mining.

Oral histories sit alongside government records and community conservation accounts, helping me connect lived experience with documentation.
